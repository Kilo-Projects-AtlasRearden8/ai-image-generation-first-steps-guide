Aspect ratios

Aspect ratio is the shape of your image — how wide it is compared to how tall it is. It is written as two numbers, like 1:1 (square) or 16:9 (wide).

Picking the right shape before you generate saves you from cropping later.

Common aspect ratios

RatioShapeBest for
1:1SquareSocial media posts, profile pictures, testing prompts
3:2Slightly widePhotographs, prints
4:3Classic photoSlideshows, older screens
16:9WideYouTube thumbnails, desktop wallpapers, cinematic scenes
9:16TallPhone wallpapers, TikTok, Reels, Stories
2:3TallPortrait posters, phone screens
21:9UltrawideSweeping landscapes, panoramas

How to set it

Most web tools have a dropdown or buttons labeled "Aspect ratio," "Size," or "Shape." Pick one before you hit generate.

Some tools only offer square images on the free plan. If you need a different shape, you can crop the image afterward using your phone's photo editor or a free online crop tool.

Local tools

If you run Stable Diffusion, Fooocus, or ComfyUI on your own computer, you set the width and height in pixels instead of a ratio. Common sizes:

  • 512 × 512 (square, 1:1)
  • 768 × 768 (square, higher detail)
  • 1024 × 1024 (square, high detail)
  • 1024 × 576 (wide, 16:9)
  • 576 × 1024 (tall, 9:16)

Larger images take longer to generate and use more of your computer's memory. Start with 512 × 512 or 1024 × 1024.

A quick rule of thumb

  • Posting to Instagram or Facebook? Use 1:1.
  • Making a phone wallpaper? Use 9:16.
  • Making a YouTube thumbnail? Use 16:9.
  • Just testing a prompt? Use 1:1 — it is the fastest and most common option.
