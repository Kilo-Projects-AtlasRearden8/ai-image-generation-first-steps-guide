Copyright basics

Copyright is the legal right that decides who can copy, share, sell, or change a creative work. AI images are new enough that the rules are still being worked out in many countries, including the United States. This page covers the basics in plain English. It is education only, not legal advice. If you plan to sell AI images or use them in a business, talk to a lawyer.

In the United States

The U.S. Copyright Office has said that works created entirely by AI — with no human authorship — generally cannot be copyrighted. That means:

  • If you type a prompt and the AI makes the image, you likely do not own a copyright in that image under current U.S. guidance.
  • Others may be able to copy, share, or sell your AI image without your permission.
  • If you significantly edit or combine AI images yourself, the parts you created by hand may be protected, but the AI-generated parts may not be.

Rules are changing, and courts are still deciding cases. What is true today might shift.

Outside the United States

Every country has its own copyright laws. Some countries may protect AI images differently, or not at all. If you live outside the U.S., check your local laws or ask a local lawyer.

What this means for beginners

  • Do not assume you own an AI image just because you prompted it.
  • Do not put AI images in a book, product, or portfolio you plan to sell without checking the rules first.
  • If you need full ownership (for example, illustrations for a children's book you will publish on KDP), consider hiring an illustrator or making the art yourself.

Tool terms of service

Even if copyright is unclear, each tool has its own rules about what you can do with the images you make:

  • Some free tools let you use images for personal projects but require a paid plan for commercial use.
  • Some give you full rights to images you generate.
  • Some keep rights for themselves.

Read the terms of service of the tool you use. "Commercial use" generally means using an image to make money — selling prints, putting it on a book cover, or using it in ads.

Being respectful

  • Do not prompt the AI to copy a specific living artist's style ("in the style of [artist]"). It is legal gray area at best, and many artists find it hurtful.
  • Do not claim an AI image is a photograph of a real event. That spreads misinformation.
  • Do not generate images of real people without their consent.

Bottom line

Treat AI images as something you can enjoy and share, but not something you automatically own outright. When in doubt, assume you cannot exclusively control an AI image, and get legal advice before using one commercially.
