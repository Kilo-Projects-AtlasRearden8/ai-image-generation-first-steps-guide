Local vs cloud

You can make AI images in two main ways: in the cloud (a website) or locally (on your own computer). Neither is "better" — they serve different needs. This page helps you pick.

Cloud: websites and apps

Cloud tools run on someone else's computer. You open a website, type a prompt, and the picture appears in your browser.

Examples: Bing Image Creator, Leonardo.ai, SeaArt.ai, Civitai

Good things:

  • Nothing to install. Works on any device with a browser.
  • Fast results, even on an old laptop or phone.
  • The service handles updates and new models for you.

Things to know:

  • Your prompt and images go to the service's servers. Read their privacy policy if that matters to you.
  • Free plans usually limit how many images you can make per day.
  • You need an internet connection.
  • The service can change its rules, models, or prices at any time.

Local: software on your computer

Local tools run on your own machine. Nothing leaves your computer unless you choose to share it.

Examples: Stable Diffusion, Fooocus, ComfyUI

Good things:

  • Private. Your prompts and images stay on your computer.
  • No daily limits. Make as many images as you want.
  • No internet needed after you download the model.
  • You own the setup. No company can take it away.

Things to know:

  • You must download and install software (often a few gigabytes).
  • You need a decent computer, especially a graphics card (GPU), for fast results. Older computers can still run small models, but slowly.
  • You are responsible for updates and troubleshooting.
  • Some models have safety rules built in, but you control what runs on your machine.

My suggestion

Start with a cloud tool to learn how prompts work. If you find yourself making lots of images, want privacy, or do not want to worry about daily limits, try a local tool like Fooocus or Stable Diffusion.

You can use both. Many people use cloud tools for quick ideas and local tools for bigger projects.
