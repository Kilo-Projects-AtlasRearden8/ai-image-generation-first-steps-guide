# Safety filters

AI image tools have safety filters — automatic rules that block or blur images that break the tool's rules. Filters exist to stop hate, harassment, violence, sexual content, and images of real people made without consent.

They are not perfect. Sometimes they block something harmless, and sometimes they miss something they should catch. This page explains how they work and what to do when they get in your way.

## Why filters exist

Image models are trained on billions of pictures from the internet, including some that are harmful. Without filters, the models could generate hate symbols, graphic violence, non-consensual images of real people, or sexual content. Tool makers add filters to keep the service safe and legal for everyone.

## What usually gets blocked

Different tools have different rules, but most block:

- Hate speech and symbols
- Harassment or bullying
- Graphic violence or gore
- Sexual content and nudity
- Images of real people (especially celebrities or private individuals) made without consent
- Self-harm instructions or imagery

## If your prompt is blocked

That does not mean you did anything wrong. It often means one word tripped the filter. Try these steps:

1. **Change one word at a time.** Instead of "blood," try "red." Instead of "weapon," try "sword" or "wand" if it fits your scene.
2. **Simplify the prompt.** Remove details until the image generates, then add them back one by one to find the offending word.
3. **Pick a different subject.** A "dark forest" is fine; a "dark forest with a corpse" may be blocked.
4. **Try a different tool.** Each service sets its own filter strictness. One tool may block a prompt that another allows.

## If a filter blocks something harmless

This is called a false positive, and it is common. Words like "naked," "shot," "kill," or "bomb" can trigger filters even in innocent contexts ("naked mole rat," "shot of espresso," "kill two birds with one stone," "bath bomb"). Rephrase using synonyms:

- "naked mole rat" → "hairless rodent"
- "shot of espresso" → "espresso close-up"
- "kill two birds with one stone" → "solve two problems at once"

## What filters cannot do

Filters do not catch everything. You may still see images that are misleading, stereotypical, or low quality. You are responsible for what you create and share. Do not generate images intended to deceive, harass, or harm.

## Privacy and filters

Cloud tools review some prompts and images for safety, which means humans or automated systems at the service may see what you generate. If you need full privacy, use a local tool like Stable Diffusion or Fooocus, where nothing leaves your computer.

## Bottom line

Safety filters are guardrails, not a judgment of you. If a prompt is blocked, rephrase and try again. When you make images, keep them kind, honest, and respectful.
